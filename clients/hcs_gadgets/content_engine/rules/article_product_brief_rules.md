# HCS Gadgets — Article Product Brief Rules

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** 2026-07-04
**Phase:** Product Intelligence Phase 0 — PHASE H
**Status:** APPROVED — Future HCS writers must receive article-specific product briefs, not full catalogue access

---

## Purpose

The Article Product Brief is a targeted, job-specific brief for each queue article. It is generated by ORIN for each writing task and given to the writer. The writer must not have direct access to `product_catalog.json`.

The brief contains only what the writer needs to write the specific article — nothing more.

---

## Current Architecture (Problem)

The previous HCS writer system had the writer reading from `product_catalog.json` directly. This caused problems:

1. Writers saw `compare_at_price` and used it in articles (commercial field — volatile)
2. Writers saw `inventory_quantity` and wrote "200 units in stock" (stale — internal field)
3. Writers used SKU codes in articles (internal — never customer-facing)
4. Writers saw `images_count` and mentioned it in product descriptions
5. Writers did not know which products were actually relevant to their topic

---

## New Architecture (Solution)

```
Shopify Raw Product Data
↓
Product Knowledge Profile Builder (this system)
↓
Writer-Safe Public Content View (product_content_view.json)
↓
ORIN: Article topic → identify relevant products from product_content_view
↓
ORIN: Create Article-Specific Product Brief
↓
Writer receives: Article Product Brief + Article Claim Allowlist + HCS Writing Rules + HCS Design System v2
↓
Writer must NOT read product_catalog.json directly
```

## Article Product Brief — Schema

```json
{
  "brief_id": "string — ORIN-generated UUID",
  "article_topic": "string — the approved article topic",
  "article_type": "evergreen_guide | comparison | how_to | news | price_lead",
  "generated": "ISO-8601 timestamp",
  "approved_products": [
    {
      "product_id_internal": "string — Shopify product_id for lookup only",
      "product_name": "string — approved public product name",
      "product_handle": "string",
      "public_product_url": "string — verified URL",
      "product_relevance": "string — why this product is relevant to this article",
      "approved_claims": [
        {
          "claim": "string",
          "source_provenance": "string — where ORIN confirmed this claim",
          "confidence": "high | medium | low",
          "stability": "stable | volatile",
          "gate_1_truth": "PASS | FAIL",
          "gate_2_exposure": "PASS | FAIL",
          "gate_3_stability": "PASS | FAIL | CONDITIONAL",
          "gate_4_relevance": "PASS | FAIL",
          "gate_5_compliance": "PASS | FAIL_REVIEW_REQUIRED",
          "final_decision": "APPROVED | CONDITIONAL | REJECTED"
        }
      ],
      "forbidden_fields": ["sku", "inventory_quantity", "compare_at_price", ...],
      "unknown_facts": ["string — facts the writer should not assume"],
      "conflicting_facts": ["string — facts with conflicting sources that must not be used"],
      "compliance_warnings": ["string — safety claims needing human review"]
    }
  ],
  "approved_collection_urls": ["string — verified collection URLs relevant to this article"],
  "cta_url": "string — the one approved CTA URL for this article",
  "article_claim_allowlist": ["string — all claims approved for this article"],
  "writer_instructions": {
    "do_not_use_direct_catalog": true,
    "do_not_invent_facts": true,
    "price_requires_separate_approval": true,
    "use_approved_claims_only": true
  }
}
```

## ORIN's Brief Generation Process

For each queue job, ORIN must:

### Step 1 — Identify Relevant Products
- Read the article topic and type from the job card
- Query `product_content_view.json` for products matching the topic
- Identify which products are genuinely relevant (not just tangentially related)
- **Never include all 93 products** — scope to topic-relevant products only

### Step 2 — Determine Approved Claims
- For each relevant product, read its Product Knowledge Profile
- Apply the Five-Gate Claim Test to each potential claim
- Record the result for each claim
- Flag any claims requiring human review

### Step 3 — Build the Brief
- Compile the Article Product Brief using the schema above
- Include only approved claims (`final_decision: APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`)
- Clearly mark compliance warnings
- Provide the verified product URLs
- Include the single approved CTA URL from the link map

### Step 4 — Deliver to Writer
- Attach the brief to the job card
- Writer reads the brief + HCS Writing Rules + HCS Design System v2
- Writer does NOT receive `product_catalog.json` access

---

## Writer Constraints

The writer must:

| Must | Must Not |
|------|---------|
| Read the Article Product Brief | Read `product_catalog.json` directly |
| Use approved claims only | Invent facts not in the brief |
| Use verified product URLs | Fabricate product URLs |
| Use approved CTA URL | Change the CTA URL |
| Flag unknown facts | Guess or infer facts |
| Pass all Five Gates for each claim | Add claims not in the allowlist |

---

## Price in Product Briefs

**Price is handled separately from the standard brief.**

If the article is explicitly price-led (e.g., "Best hoverboards under £100"), the brief must request a current price fetch:
1. ORIN flags the job as requiring price data
2. A separate verified price lookup is performed at time of writing
3. Current prices are added to the brief as a separate `price_data` section
4. Prices are marked as `volatile — confirm at time of publication`

Price must never be copied from `product_catalog.json` directly.

---

## Unknown Facts Protocol

When a writer encounters a question the brief doesn't answer:

1. **Do not guess.** Record `unknown` in the article
2. Flag the gap in the draft for ORIN review
3. ORIN either: confirms the fact is unknown and should remain absent, or provides a verified answer
4. The writer updates the article with the verified answer only

**MISSING DATA != PERMISSION TO INFER.**

---

## Brief ID and Audit Trail

Every brief has a unique `brief_id` (UUID) for audit purposes. ORIN logs each brief generation.

If an article is challenged for accuracy, the brief ID allows reconstruction of exactly what ORIN approved for the writer at the time.
